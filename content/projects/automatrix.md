## The problem

Hosting prices are not comparable. The sticker omits egress, the IPv4 address, the renewal after
the promo window, and whether the meter runs per workflow, per task, or per credit. Every "cheap VPS"
list online compares the number that is easiest to scrape instead of the number that shows up on the
invoice.

## What I built

An audited price index, published as dated editions (currently 2026-Q4) rather than a live monitor,
because a monitor across eleven vendors makes every figure unfalsifiable after the fact.

- **Traceability**: every plan record stores `source_url`, a verbatim quote from the vendor page, the date it was read, and whether it was read directly or through a rendering proxy. The quote is expandable on the page and its sha256 is published.
- **Null is not zero**: an unpublished rate is `null`, never `0`. A plan that prices a term but not the rate after it refuses to compute a 36-month total.
- **Arithmetic that fails the build**: converted prices must match `native_price × fx_rate` within two cents, the FX date must match the FX file, and every category needs exactly three featured plans. The build stops otherwise.
- **The audits**: three live audits (compute, coding agents, automation), each provider scored on compute, egress, ipv4, storage and contract. `not_published` is treated as a finding, not a gap in the work.
- **Output as data**: the site renders from the same records it publishes, so the prose cannot drift from the numbers. The dataset ships with a JSON Schema (2020-12) and a verification manifest.

## How it is held honest

A CI gate validates the schema, the five-vector coverage and the arithmetic; another checks the built
dataset against its own published JSON Schema; a third re-checks weekly that every cited URL still
resolves. The static output is generated by a script and deployed as a Cloudflare Worker with the
archive embedded, since Pages direct-upload cannot be driven without custom headers.

The whole argument is one table: eleven compute plans, 36-month totals at 1 TB and 5 TB a month. The
cheapest sticker is the cheapest bill at every traffic level I priced. The second-cheapest is not.
